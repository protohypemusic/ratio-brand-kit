# How Ratio writes

These rules apply to every email, report, memo, letter and slide a client or
prospect reads. They are the same rules the client portal uses when it drafts
the monthly client report.

## Who we are

- Ratio is a **full-service accounting partner**. The line that sums it up:
  "the Ratio team pays for itself."
- **Never** call Ratio a "virtual CFO" or a "bookkeeping service".
- **Never** claim MBE or WBE certification.

## Voice

- Write to the client directly: "your revenue", "your margin held at 75%".
  Never third person ("the client's revenue").
- Warm but professional. This is a note from their accountant, not from a
  friend. A short greeting, a light seasonal line where it fits naturally
  ("Hope the holiday weekend treated you well"), a warm sign-off.
- Confident and direct. When a number is good, say so in plain words and let
  the figure carry it: "September was your strongest month of the year."
- Coach, don't just report. When something is off, pair it with a benchmark
  or a dollar impact AND a next step: "gross profit slipped to about 72%, you
  should be closer to 75%, so let's dig in."
- When something looks bad but isn't, say why: "payroll is up, but that's the
  year-end bonuses, not a concern."
- If a figure would mislead (a one-time item, a lumpy posting), say you are
  setting it aside rather than burying it in caveats.
- Close with a next step (offer a call, ask for a document, name a date).

## Banned

- **No em-dashes (—) or en-dashes (–) as punctuation.** Use a comma, a colon,
  or two sentences. An em-dash is the clearest sign a machine wrote it. A
  hyphen in a number range ("Jan-Mar") is fine.
- No sports metaphors, slang or hype: "crushed it", "knocked it out of the
  park", "killing it", "slam dunk", "home run", "on fire", "rockstar",
  "game changer". The list is examples, not the whole of it.
- No filler: "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "I hope this email
  finds you well", "in today's fast-paced world".
- No adverb padding: really, just, simply, actually, very, truly.
- No exclamation points in client reports. One, at most, in a friendly email.

## Numbers

- Use only figures that come from the source (the P&L, the return, the bank
  statement). Never invent, estimate or project a number that is not there.
- Round the way a person would: "about $64K", "roughly 72%", "close to $60K".
- Always say the period: "in September", "over the last twelve months".
- Never annualize a partial year or multiply a month by twelve unless asked,
  and say so when you do.
- Never write a full SSN, EIN or account number. Last four digits only
  ("SSN ending 1234").

## Shape of a client update email

1. Greeting by first name, one warm line.
2. The month: revenue, gross profit, payroll, net income, as short points,
   each with the number and a plain-English read, compared to last month and
   the same month last year where available.
3. The last twelve months as the business's current annual pace, with light
   coaching where a number is off benchmark.
4. Anything to watch, each with a benchmark or dollar impact and a next step.
5. A forward step (offer a call) and a warm sign-off with name and title.

Section labels on their own line, no colon: "September", "Last twelve
months", "Projects".
